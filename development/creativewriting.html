In class, in week 7, Willow and I had a discussion to check in about my work and where I was headed. I felt pretty happy with how things were progressing, and was able to share some inital physical trials of the sunprinting. However, I knew that I needed to seek some guidance on what my actual content should be. What 'immortal story' will I actually use for the words on my dress and in my book? I had considered using the most obvious ones that are in the public domain - The Odyssey, Hamlet, or a version of one of the other stories from The Hero with a Thousand Faces. I also considered looking to the Regency era and Jane Austen, since that would align with the style of dress that I was producing. But none of these felt that interesting, that fresh, or quite right. I explained all this to Willow and she had the brilliant suggestion that I write my own story.

This was a definite turning point in my development, where I came to understand how I would reinvent the hero's journey/coming of age story. I would tell my own, and in fact I already had told it. Two years ago, I wrote a short story called Scratch Scratch, that was about my then very disordered relationship with my body. I think it took me a long time, and a couple of bad romantic/sexual experiences to be able to view my body with respect, and I of course still have to work on this every day. But at the time of writing Scratch Scratch, this self destructive impluse was very strong. I felt very sad and very confused and alone. So, when Willow suggested that I write my own version of the hero's journey, I realised that I already had the worlds on the page. This short story would form the content of my project.
