Now that all three pieces of my fabric were treated, it was time to wait for a sunny day. When one rolled around, I took advantage and exposed my anthotypes and cyanotype. On my beetroot dyed fabric, I wanted to retain as much of the really vibrant pink as possible, meaning that I needed to cover as much of the piece as possible. For this reason, I used the mdf boards from which my letters had been cut, rather than the letters. I laid down some blankets to protect the fabric and then put out my fabric. I then quickly positioned my mdf boards and my mdf needle, not worrying too much about the composition, and left the fabric to expose in the sun. It was outside for around three hours in UV 5-9 sun.
