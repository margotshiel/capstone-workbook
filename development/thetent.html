It's weird, but I knew that I would have to get to the end of this process before I could get clarity on exactly what it is that I am exploring here. And it was in the last week, the final push, when I drafted my third visualisation of the exhibition setup that I actually understood this work as an extension of a piece I did in May this year. This installation was called The Tent and I built it for my Interactive Media class. This is what I wrote about that project at the time:

When presented with the opportunity to create any kind of expansive, experimental, interactive media artwork that I could dream of, I was overwhelmed and excited. The skills I have learnt in coding, physical computing and interactive art as whole, have been truly empowering, and have opened an entirely new world of fabrication, prototyping and production. So, the task of distilling all this magic in just a few weeks seemed almost impossible.

Being at the end of my degree and facing the wide-open world (and world wide web), also makes me feel more scared and more alive than anything I have experienced before. I wanted to capture this feeling of magic, of the world around me expanding in ways I cannot understand; the feeling of endless possibilities and uncertainty and the unknown. But at the same time, I wanted relief from my fears and anxieties about the future, and so, I looked to my childhood, where fairies and spells were real, and the world felt warm and safe.

As a child, I made potions and concocted enchanting myths in my fairy garden. I whispered secrets to the flowers and my soft toys, and the tiny trinkets that filled my jewellery boxes. Reading and poetry were another form of this quiet contemplation. My approach to this project, therefore, was to aim to conjure feelings of wonder, mystery and magic, wrapped up in feelings of safety and comfort. I wanted to create an opportunity for people to return to their childhood selves and remember how it feels to believe in magic.

I think that I had unintentionally been circling these same feelings and desires, and continuing my research into childhood stories, and my understanding of that time, who I was then and who I am now.
