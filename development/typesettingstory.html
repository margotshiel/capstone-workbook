Once I had my typeface finalised, I created a digital plan of how I wanted to print my fabric. In illustrator, I created artboards in the size of my fabric pieces and began playing around with typesetting my short story using my custom typeface. I knew that to create my stencils I wanted to lasercut mdf boards at my work at the Uni Melb Creator Space, and so I was also working within the restrictions of 900mmx600mm blocks, as this is the size of the laser cutter beds. This was all happening at the busiest time in The Creator Space, and the level of detail I wanted on my boards was going to take a long time to cut out.

So keeping all of this in mind, I set my text to fill a single panel of fabric and then manipulated it using the mesh warp tool. I knew that I would not be able to print three sets of stencils, and so I settled for printing one, and then using the inverse cut out letters on my second piece of fabric. Since cyanotypes have the shortest processing time, I decided to replicate a technique I used in Woman and Dog, and lay on the fabric in the sunshine, in order to print my own body onto the fabric.
