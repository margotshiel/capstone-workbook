I was able to utilise my Brief 1 - Type Specimen to test another typogaphic/textile technique. I chose the pixel based Neuebit from Pangram Pangram, as I wanted to explore the similarities between pixel based images and many textiles patterns/guides. Cross stitching patterns are made up of a grid of squares that the embroiderer fills with different colours to create images. I was inpsired by the work of Susan Kare, the designer of the original Apple icons. Her research and early sketches directly reference cross stitching, and so I thought it would be fun to take a pixel font and move it back into the analogue.

I trialed my cross stitching technique and ended up using a water soluble stabiliser to print my guide onto, as well as stabilising the slippery silk fabric. I really love how this piece came out, and I think the technique creates a version of the typeface that is pretty faithful to the digital original. I am keeping this in mind as I move towards the final piece, as an option to add texture and depth - but of course this would require a pixel based typeface. The final relevant thing from this project is that the words I embroidered on the dress are an extract from a William Wordsworth poem, My Heart Leaps Up When I Behold, which I dicuss further in my research entry on the poet. This sense of joy and wonder in the natural world is something I wanted to continue exploring in my final piece.
