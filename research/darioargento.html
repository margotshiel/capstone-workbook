In my Image and Media Wayposts in 2024, I included Peter Sobczynski's writing on Dario Argento's Suspiria. This paragraph is a pretty perfect summary of my thinking about the movie and what makes it so inspiring to me. I went back and rewatched the film as a part of my research for this capstone project, and I was again, struck and inspired by the treatment of sound, colour and lighting to make a dreamlike/uncanny come to life. Here's the quote from Peter Sobczynski:

Argento further develops the film’s dreamlike nature by utilising a truly go-for-baroque approach that takes all of the stylistic tricks at his disposal and pushes them to their limits and beyond. One of his key collaborators is cinematographer Luciano Tovoli, whose previous efforts, such as Michelangelo Antonioni’s The Passenger(1975), were marked by a down-to-earth realism. In Suspiria, he went as far in the other direction as possible. In this film’s unearthly visual scheme, mirrors, filters, enormous arc lights and offbeat colours are accentuated by way of Technicolor’s famous three-colour dye transfer process. The vivid hues combine with a constantly moving camera to create one of the most distinct-looking films of any genre to emerge during the period.

Adding to the surreal atmosphere is the booming score composed and performed by prog-rock group Goblin, who created rough versions of their music for Argento to play on the set while shooting scenes in which they would be featured. Another oddball touch: virtually the entire film was dubbed in post-production with no actual live sound remaining. Granted, this was the norm for the Italian film industry at the time, where it was deemed easier to just simply redub films that try to record them live, but it does add an additional bit of weirdness that viewers pick up on even if they don’t know precisely what’s unsettling them.
