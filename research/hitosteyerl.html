I read through Hito Steyerl's In Defense of the Poor Image and while I agree with basically everything that she is saying, and also am a profound enjoyer of the poor, pixelated, or degraded image, I also did not necessarily find this piece super exciting or eye opening. Maybe this is because of the time that has passed since it was written (2009), and the fact that our understanding of digital images has changed so vastly.

The idea that I find the most interesting is that a poor image is a popular image - one that has been seen and shared by many, and thus democratised and openly available. I like the idea that each person's sharing or compressing of an image is like putting ther digitial finger prints or signature on the image - they are changing or reinventing the 'original' in some way, and the outcome is thus a collective creation/effort.
