During my experiment with cyanotypes, I have also come across references to 'anthotypes', which are sun prints that use plant based pigments rather than chemical compounds. The most common two that I have seen are turmeric and beetroot. I began by looking into beetroot, andd found this wonderful blog post where a producer experimented with different levels of turmeric, different fixatives and different process chemical aides in her anthotypes. In order to work well with the brilliant blue of the cyanotypes, I thought that a bright yellow would look best, rather than the kind of burnt orange that can be achieved with a baking soda fixative. This also gave me some indication about processing times and after care that I took with me into my experimentation and eventual use of turmeric anthotypes.

The information that I took the most note of here was that processing times were generally >3hrs and in high UV. I also took note that the areas exposed to the sun became lighter, and those covered remained dark. This is the opposite to cyanotypes and so was something I made sure to keep in mind when planning designs. See the entries discussing this technique further on my development page.
